Part 2 — Matrix Setup and Standardization

pred_cols = ["size_sqft", "bedrooms", "age_years"]

predictors = df[pred_cols].to_numpy()
y = df["price_thousands"].to_numpy()

means = predictors.mean(axis=0)
sds = predictors.std(axis=0)

Z = (predictors - means) / sds

Written response. X and y must have compatible shapes because the model y ≈ Xβ requires the number of rows in X to equal the number of observations in y. Otherwise X @ beta and the residuals y - X @ beta cannot be computed. The intercept column of ones lets the model estimate a baseline level (the predicted price when all standardized predictors are at their means) instead of forcing the fitted surface through the origin. Standardizing put all three predictors on a common scale with mean 0 and standard deviation 1, so their coefficients can be compared directly. Broadcasting makes this possible without a loop because NumPy stretches the length-3 vectors means and sds across every row of the (n, 3) matrix, so (predictors - means) / sds is applied to each column at once.
